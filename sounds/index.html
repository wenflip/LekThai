The tones haven’t been checked by a native Thai speaker yet, and the audio is your phone’s built-in voice. Trust your ears, and expect fixes.

How to play

Tap Play. You’ll hear one Thai word. Tap the one you think you heard. You don’t need to know any Thai — the meanings are only there to make each sound memorable.

After you answer, tap each row to hear them side by side. That comparison is where your ear actually learns, so don’t rush past it.

Each round is 10 words. Start with 2 tones and move up when you’re getting most of them right.

Why this works

Thai uses pitch (tone) and vowel length to tell words apart. English doesn’t, so English speakers’ ears skip right over them. The good news: adults can retrain this.

The best-studied method is exactly this game: hear a sound, choose, get instant feedback, repeat with many different words. Research on Thai tones found English speakers improved after just a few days of short training sessions.

Hearing comes first. You can’t say a tone you can’t hear, and you can’t check yourself either.
