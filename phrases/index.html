How to use it

① Hear & Choose plays a Thai phrase. Work out what it means in your head first, then show the choices and tap the one that matches. Start here: understanding comes before speaking. Some phrases are ones Thais say to you, like “Do you need a bag?” — this is where you learn those.

② See & Say describes a situation. Say the Thai out loud from memory, then tap reveal to hear it and check yourself — including the tones. Be honest: a wrong tone can change the meaning.

Polite ending: Thais end most sentences with ครับ (men) or ค่ะ (women; คะ for questions). Turn it on in settings and the app adds yours to every phrase you say.

Don’t guess. If you don’t know it, tap No idea or I don’t know. A lucky guess tells the app you know a phrase you don’t, so it shows it to you less. An honest “don’t know” keeps it coming back, and it won’t break your ⚡ streak.

The first time you meet a phrase you won’t know it — that’s fine, the reveal is the lesson. It only counts as a miss from the second time on.

Why this works

LekThai is built on the two most strongly evidenced ideas in learning science: retrieval practice and spaced repetition.

Every prompt makes you recall the answer before you see it — say it aloud, or work out the meaning — instead of just reading along. The effort of pulling an answer from memory is what builds the memory; passive review barely moves the needle.

It also remembers how you do. Phrases you miss come back sooner and more often; phrases you know fade into occasional check-ins, and come back more the longer it’s been. A few focused minutes a day beats one long cram.
